## 1.0 Summary

Severe hypertriglyceridemia means very high levels of triglycerides, a type of fat in the blood, and it raises the risk of acute pancreatitis, a sudden inflammation of the pancreas. In two identical placebo-controlled trials, SHASTA-3 and SHASTA-4, published in the New England Journal of Medicine, 757 adults with the condition received plozasiran 25 mg or placebo every three months. At 12 months, median triglycerides were 79% and 81% below baseline in the plozasiran groups. More than 9 in 10 treated patients were below 500 mg/dL, compared with about half of those on placebo. Combining both trials, acute pancreatitis occurred at a rate of 1 per 100 patient-years with plozasiran and 5 per 100 patient-years with placebo (rate ratio 0.22). Adverse events were reported in 73% of patients in both groups. In the United States, plozasiran is approved only for familial chylomicronemia syndrome, a rare inherited form of the disorder, so using it for the much more common forms studied here would be off-label. This summary is based on the published abstract, which gives no detailed safety data. The manufacturer funded both trials.

## 2.0 Key Points

1. SHASTA-3 and SHASTA-4 randomly assigned 757 adults with severe hypertriglyceridemia, in a 2:1 ratio, to plozasiran 25 mg or placebo at baseline and at months 3, 6 and 9 [1].
2. At month 12, median fasting triglycerides fell 79% and 81% with plozasiran. Compared with placebo, the differences were -45 percentage points (95% CI -51 to -38) and -47 percentage points (95% CI -54 to -39) [1].
3. A triglyceride level below 500 mg/dL at 12 months was reached by 91% and 93% of patients on plozasiran, compared with 51% and 50% on placebo (P<0.001) [1].
4. In the pooled analysis, adjudicated acute pancreatitis occurred at 1 per 100 patient-years with plozasiran and 5 per 100 patient-years with placebo (rate ratio 0.22; P=0.008) [1].
5. Adverse events during treatment occurred in 73% of patients in both pooled groups. The abstract does not break these down, and the trials were funded by Arrowhead Pharmaceuticals [1].

## 3.0 Evidence base

Triglycerides are carried in the blood by particles made in the gut and the liver. A protein called apolipoprotein C-III (apoC-III) slows their removal. Plozasiran is a small interfering RNA, a drug that destroys the genetic message liver cells use to make apoC-III. With less apoC-III, triglycerides are cleared from the blood faster [2]. It is given as an injection every three months [1].

SHASTA-3 and SHASTA-4 had identical designs, end points and analysis plans [1]. Together they enrolled 757 adults with severe hypertriglyceridemia. Two patients received plozasiran 25 mg for every one who received placebo, with doses at baseline and at months 3, 6 and 9 [1].

The main measure was the percent change in fasting triglycerides from baseline to month 12. The secondary measures included the change at month 10, the share of patients who got below 500 mg/dL and below 150 mg/dL, the change in patients who started at 880 mg/dL or higher, and changes in remnant cholesterol and non-HDL cholesterol. A separate analysis pooled the two trials to look at acute pancreatitis, confirmed by an independent committee [1].

At month 12, median triglycerides were 79% lower than baseline with plozasiran in one trial and 81% lower in the other. Compared with placebo, the differences were 45 percentage points (95% CI 38 to 51) and 47 percentage points (95% CI 39 to 54) [1]. These differences are smaller than the raw drop because triglycerides also fell in the placebo groups. The abstract does not say why.

The 500 mg/dL threshold is the level this site's model uses to mark pancreatitis risk. At 12 months, 91% and 93% of patients on plozasiran were below that level, compared with 51% and 50% on placebo (P<0.001) [1].

In the pooled analysis, acute pancreatitis occurred at a rate of 1 per 100 patient-years with plozasiran and 5 per 100 patient-years with placebo. That rate ratio of 0.22 means the rate on plozasiran was about one fifth of the rate on placebo (P=0.008) [1].

Adverse events were reported in 73% of patients in both pooled groups [1]. The abstract does not say which events these were.

## 4.0 Where it fits in practice

The Sandusky model treats a fasting triglyceride level of 500 mg/dL or higher as a pancreatitis problem first, and it is one of the reasons for referral to the clinic ({% link clinical/02-patient-eligibility.md %}). In the model, plozasiran sits in the pancreatitis-prevention part of the pathway, for familial chylomicronemia syndrome (FCS) ({% link clinical/05-treatment-pathways.md %}).

The US Food and Drug Administration approved plozasiran on November 18, 2025, as an addition to diet for adults with FCS [2]. FCS is rare. Most people with triglycerides above 500 mg/dL do not have it, and their high levels come from several causes acting together. SHASTA-3 and SHASTA-4 studied this larger group, and their results, including the reduction in pancreatitis, are now published [1]. Our [September post]({% link lipidology/2026-09-17-plozasiran-redemplo-is-approved-for-an-inherited-triglycerid.md %}) covered these trials when only conference news was available.

For now, nothing changes in routine prescribing. In patients without FCS, plozasiran remains off-label in the United States until the approved use is widened. If that happens, the patients these trials describe are adults whose triglycerides stay above 500 mg/dL.

These trials measured triglycerides and pancreatitis. They did not test whether plozasiran prevents heart attacks or strokes. In the model, lowering heart risk still depends on LDL cholesterol and apolipoprotein B.

## 5.0 Open questions

This summary rests on the abstract. It reports the shared adverse event rate but not which events occurred, and it gives no results for liver tests, blood sugar, platelets or LDL cholesterol [1]. Those details matter because earlier plozasiran trials raised questions about blood sugar control and, at the highest dose, LDL cholesterol, as described in our [September post]({% link lipidology/2026-09-17-plozasiran-redemplo-is-approved-for-an-inherited-triglycerid.md %}). These trials used the 25 mg dose.

The abstract lists remnant cholesterol and non-HDL cholesterol as secondary measures but does not give the results [1]. The same goes for the below-150 mg/dL target and the subgroup with very high starting levels.

The pancreatitis finding comes from pooling the two trials. The abstract gives the rates and the P value, but not the number of events or a confidence interval for the rate ratio, and follow-up was 12 months [1]. It also does not describe patients' starting triglyceride levels, their background treatment, or how many had had pancreatitis before.

Arrowhead Pharmaceuticals, the manufacturer, funded both trials [1]. Whether plozasiran affects cardiovascular events in any group of patients has not been tested.
